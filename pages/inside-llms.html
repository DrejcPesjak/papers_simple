See what is simplified ↗
What is actually being calculated?

At the tracked final position, attention gives the country a hand-set relevance score of 3 and each of the other four words a score of 0. Softmax turns these into weights: a = exp(3) / (exp(3) + 4) ≈ 0.834. Each attention step adds a × country vector to the state. Each MLP step adds 0.8 × max(0, country coordinate) to the associated answer coordinate. The readout applies softmax to the Paris and Tokyo coordinates. Bar scale: 0 to 2.1, shared across all four coordinates. The weights and associations were written by hand; nothing here was trained.

Keep this idea

The model keeps adding to a shared state. Researchers call this the residual stream. A circuit is a route through these contributions, not another box in the machine.

What a pattern-aware decoder recovers
Place

The raw coordinates do not say “place.” A decoder can still recognize the pattern. Hard for us to read does not mean unusable by the network.

Overlapping storage is called superposition. It lets networks use shared coordinates efficiently. A neuron’s activity may look noisy to us because it mixes several features—not because the model has lost the information.

Illustrative prompts and feature directions, not measured LLM activations. The miniature decoder knows eight possible on/off combinations. Real networks learn ways to use overlapping signals; interference can still occur, especially when many features activate together.

Show the geometry—and how researchers untangle real signals

The hand-chosen directions are Place = (0.8, 0.3), Language = (−0.4, 0.8), and Travel = (0.5, −0.6). The live signal is their sum. The decoder compares that sum with the eight possible on/off combinations and picks the nearest by squared distance. All eight sums are distinct, so it can recover them exactly. It receives the coordinates, not the switches. This deliberately limited decoder illustrates readable patterns; it is not a trained neural network. Two coordinates cannot uniquely recover three arbitrary continuous feature strengths.

Researchers use sparse autoencoders to find recurring directions in real model activity. These tools learn to reconstruct activity using a small selection from a larger dictionary of patterns. Many patterns are understandable, but the dictionary is neither complete nor a proven list of the model’s “atoms of thought.” This demo does not train an autoencoder.

03

Break it. Then explain it.

To find what causes an answer, change something inside. But watch out: another route can pick up the work.

Paper §7, 52, 66–68 ↗
A tiny circuit with a backup
Combined output signal
1.00
Intact reference: 1.00

A normalized toy signal, not an LLM’s accuracy or confidence.

Two routes. One answer.

The main route supplies most of the signal. The backup adds a little. Cut the main route to test whether its contribution matters.

Before you cut: will losing the main route kill the output?

Why this matters: an unchanged answer does not prove a component was unimportant. And a successful patch shows a causal contribution in this setup—not that you found the only route.

Toy rules, openly stated ↗
See the calculation behind the experiment

Input signal is fixed at 1. The main route gives m = 0.8 when intact or patched, otherwise 0. If the backup is enabled, it adds b = max(0, 1 − m); otherwise b = 0. Output is m + b. Thus the four trials produce 1.00, 1.00, 0.00, and 0.80. Patching copies the saved main activation from the intact run into the damaged run.

The compensating rule is deliberately programmed to illustrate self-repair, not fitted to measured LLM behavior. Real backup mechanisms are more varied. Real patches can also create unnatural internal states, so researchers need further tests.

The distinction

Seeing a signal suggests information is there. Changing it and testing the result asks whether the model actually uses it.

Step back / How far does the map reach?

A finding comes with a boundary.

The paper gathers 90 findings. These examples show why “we understand this mechanism” and “we understand the whole model” are very different claims.

High · within scopeSome small circuits are mapped in detail.
What was found

In GPT-2 Small, researchers traced a name-selection behavior to 26 attention heads. They tested how the parts work together by intervening on them.

Paper §6 · page 6 ↗
Where the claim stops

This explains a controlled family of sentences in a particular model. It is not a full explanation of language, reasoning, or every larger model.

High · existenceRecognizable concepts can be extracted.
What was found

Sparse autoencoders recover many interpretable patterns, including in production-scale models. Increasing some of these signals changes behavior.

Paper §16–19 · pages 10–11 ↗
Where the claim stops

A useful dictionary is not necessarily complete or unique. A named feature is not automatically a single, indivisible thought.

Medium · case studiesSome reasoning happens before the words.
What was found

Claude 3.5 Haiku case studies traced intermediate factual steps and future rhyme endings before the model printed them.

Paper §29–31 · page 15 ↗
Where the claim stops

A written explanation can reflect internal computation or justify an answer reached another way. It is not a guaranteed transcript—and these cases do not tell us how often each happens.

Causal · limited scopeInternal controls can change behavior.
What was found

Researchers can steer some behaviors by changing activation directions, and edit certain factual associations by changing weights.

Paper §53–62, 88 · pages 24–27, 36 ↗
Where the claim stops

A working control is not a complete explanation. Strong steering can distort behavior; repeated factual edits can damage other abilities.

Preliminary / mixedA universal “mind reader” does not exist.
What was found

Some studies detect evaluation-related signals, deliberately trained hidden behaviors, and limited self-report of injected internal concepts.

Paper §40–50, 84 · pages 19–23, 35 ↗
Where the claim stops

The evidence varies by study. Finding an implanted backdoor is easier than detecting arbitrary deception. Limited self-report does not establish human-like awareness or feelings.

The paper’s big idea

Readable pieces.
Unfinished map.

Language models compute with meaningful internal signals. Those signals overlap, travel through circuits, and sometimes take backup routes.

We can test parts of that story. We cannot yet reconstruct the whole one.

What We Collectively Know About How LLMs Work Internally ↗

A 40-page research synthesis with sources through September 14, 2026. This page explains selected mechanisms; it does not reproduce all 90 findings. Evidence labels summarize the synthesis’s assessments, not a new independent review.

How much of this lab is real?
Computed live
Attention weights, residual additions, a rectified association transform, two-choice softmax, vector sums and an eight-pattern decoder, circuit contributions, and schematic name selection. Every changing number comes from these calculations.
Scaled down
Five whole-word tokens, one tracked token position, four coordinates, two representative layer updates (labelled 1 and N), and two answer candidates. Intervening layers are not simulated. These sizes are teaching choices.
Hand-chosen
The country-to-capital associations, attention scores, feature directions, binary feature dictionary, prompt examples, and backup rule. The feature labels are illustrative. No weights were learned and no experiment ran on a real LLM.
Left out
Training, real tokenization, normalization, full attention matrices, multiple heads, vocabulary-scale generation, and actual feature or circuit discovery. The spatial cutaway is a metaphor; it is not physical model anatomy.
